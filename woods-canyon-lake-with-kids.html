Woods Canyon Lake With Kids

Pine forest, cool air, shoreline exploring, fishing and a family day that can be as simple or active as you want it to be.

By Mark Stephenson, RNArizona father and cardiac cath lab nurseUpdated September 8, 2026

The short answer

Woods Canyon Lake is one of the easiest Arizona escapes for families who want water, pine forest and cooler Rim Country weather without committing to a difficult hike. A strong family plan is simple: arrive early, explore a manageable shoreline section, break for lunch, then choose fishing, more lake time or one nearby Mogollon Rim stop.

Why Woods Canyon Lake works so well with kids

The biggest advantage is flexibility. Families can walk part of the shoreline, fish, picnic, explore the forest or spend time near the water without turning the outing into an all-or-nothing hike. That makes it easier to adjust for younger children, tired legs or changing weather.

Hiking Woods Canyon Lake with kids

There is a trail around the lake, but families do not need to complete the full loop to have a good day. Natural shoreline terrain can include roots, rocks and uneven surfaces, so choose a turnaround point based on the youngest or least-mobile person in your group.

Health & Travels family rule: toddlers can explore a short shoreline section, younger kids can walk until interest fades, and older kids can turn the hike itself into the adventure. Nobody receives a commemorative plaque for forcing a miserable loop.

Fishing with kids

Arizona Game & Fish describes Woods Canyon as a scenic lake popular with families, with fishing opportunities for brown, rainbow and tiger trout. Check current Arizona fishing-license requirements and regulations before casting. With young children, keep hooks, line and water access under close adult supervision.

Kayaking and paddling

Small-watercraft recreation is part of the Woods Canyon experience. Bring properly fitted life jackets, watch wind and storm development, and do not let a pleasant morning trick you into assuming the afternoon will behave itself. Mountain weather has never signed a contract.

Bathrooms, picnic areas and food

Forest Service recreation information lists toilets in the Woods Canyon Lake recreation area, and developed areas include picnic and camping facilities. Because services are seasonal, confirm current conditions before the drive. For families, use the bathroom when you arrive, carry your own water and keep lunch simple.

Our favorite family-day rhythm

Lake → explore → lunch → one more activity → head home before everyone turns feral.

Eating in the middle of the outing gives children a reset and lets adults reassess weather, energy and whether the second half of the day should be fishing, another short walk or simply sitting near the water.

What to pack

  • More drinking water than you expect to use
  • Snacks and an easy lunch
  • Sunscreen and hats
  • Layers for changing temperatures
  • Closed-toe shoes with decent traction
  • Small first-aid kit
  • Wet wipes and an extra set of kid clothes
  • Offline map or saved directions
  • Fishing gear and licenses if fishing
  • Life jackets if paddling

Weather and monsoon safety

Woods Canyon Lake sits at a much higher elevation than Phoenix, which is exactly why families love it in warmer months. That also means conditions can change quickly. Summer thunderstorms can bring lightning, strong wind, hail and sudden temperature drops. Check the hourly forecast, Forest Service alerts and fire restrictions before departure, and have a firm point at which you will leave the water or trail.

Our simple Woods Canyon Lake family itinerary

8:00 AM: Leave the Phoenix area.

10:00 AM: Arrive, find the bathrooms and orient everyone before starting.

10:30 AM: Take a short shoreline walk or explore the pine forest.

11:30 AM: Fishing, paddling or relaxed lake time.

12:30 PM: Picnic lunch and weather check.

1:30 PM: One more short activity if everyone still has energy.

2:30 PM: Optional Mogollon Rim viewpoint.

3:30 PM: Head home or stop in Payson for food.
