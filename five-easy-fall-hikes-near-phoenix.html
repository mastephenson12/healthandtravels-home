5 Easy Fall Hikes Near Phoenix for Families

Five low-friction desert walks for families who want a real outing without accidentally signing up for a summit expedition.

By Mark Stephenson, RNArizona father and cardiac catheterization laboratory nurse since 2003Updated September 7, 2026

Pick by family fit, not by Instagram fame

For a cooler-season family hike near Phoenix, start with the least-experienced walker, current temperature, shade needs, bathroom needs and how much driving your group will tolerate. These five choices are intentionally easier and more adjustable than Phoenix's famous summit hikes.

September is still hot. “Fall” in Phoenix does not guarantee cool hiking weather. Check same-day official trail restrictions and the hourly forecast, start early when needed, and skip the hike if heat conditions are poor.

Best first desert hike

1. Papago Park Nature Trail

A short, easy gravel walk near picnic ramadas and a playground. City of Phoenix lists the Nature Trail at 0.5 miles with about 20 feet of elevation change, making it one of the simplest ways to give kids a real desert walk without a long commitment.

Kid difficultyVery easy
ShadeLow on trail
BathroomsPublic facilities nearby

Best West Valley pick

2. Gila Trail at Estrella Mountain Regional Park

The county lists Gila Trail as a 0.5-mile, hike-only, barrier-free route. This is the easiest choice here for West Valley families who want a defined desert walk and access to the broader park without committing to a long trail.

Kid difficultyVery easy
ShadeLow
BathroomsPark facilities
Open directionsRead Estrella guide
Best for strollers

3. Judith Tunell Accessible Trail at South Mountain

Two half-mile hard-surface loops with minimal elevation gain, benches, drinking fountains and covered ramadas. This is the strongest option when smooth footing and frequent rest opportunities matter more than mileage.

Kid difficultyVery easy
ShadeSome at ramadas
BathroomsNear education center
Open directionsFamily safety guide
Best East Valley pick

4. Merkle Trail at Usery Mountain Regional Park

Usery Mountain Regional Park describes Merkle Trail as a 1-mile barrier-free route with gentle grades. It gives families classic Sonoran Desert scenery without requiring the steeper Wind Cave climb.

Kid difficultyEasy
ShadeLow
BathroomsTrailhead area

Best for tiny legs

5. Penny Howe Barrier Free Trail at North Mountain Park

This 0.3-mile easy trail uses two small loops with interpretive plant signs. It is a sensible choice when the goal is getting young kids outside, not collecting mileage for reasons nobody under age six requested.

Before you leave

  • Check the hourly forecast and official park alerts.
  • Confirm parking and current operating hours.
  • Carry water even on short hikes.
  • Use sun protection and closed-toe shoes.
  • Turn around when the least-experienced person is done.
