One Perfect Family Day in Yuma, Arizona

Start beside the Colorado River, eat before the children crash, explore a restored desert wetland, and keep a heat-safe backup ready.

By Mark Stephenson, RNHealth & Travels family playbookUpdated September 11, 2026

The 30-second plan

Begin early at West Wetlands Park, where your family can choose walking paths, gardens, the playground, picnic space or a short look at the Colorado River. Cool off over an early lunch, then use Yuma East Wetlands as an optional wildlife-and-riverfront walk. Finish with a relaxed dinner in Yuma. In hot weather, shorten both outdoor stops and replace the afternoon wetlands walk with an indoor break.

Best fitFamilies who want an easy outdoor day rather than a demanding hike
BathroomsPlan around the developed West Wetlands facilities; confirm access on arrival

Your plug-and-play Yuma schedule

7:30–10:00 AM · West Wetlands Park

Let the family choose the morning adventure

The City of Yuma describes West Wetlands as a 110-acre park with restored native habitat and a developed upper area. Its listed amenities include walking paths, gardens, picnic tables, ramadas, a playground, pond, beach and boat ramp. Pick two simple goals—perhaps the playground and a short nature walk—and leave while everyone still has energy.

Parent check: Start with a bathroom stop. Keep children within arm’s reach near water, and treat the river as moving water even when the surface looks calm.

10:00–11:15 AM · Snack and reset

Use the car as base camp

Refill water, change wet or dusty clothes, apply sun protection and decide whether the day still has room for a second outdoor stop. This pause prevents a pleasant morning from quietly becoming a heat-and-hunger contest.

11:15 AM–12:30 PM · Early lunch

Choose an easy Yuma meal

Pick a current restaurant from the downtown Yuma area or along your route. Favor a place with food your children reliably eat, indoor seating and quick service. Verify the day’s hours before driving over; local schedules can change seasonally.

1:00–2:30 PM · Optional East Wetlands walk

Explore a restored desert river habitat

Yuma East Wetlands protects hundreds of acres of cottonwood, willow, mesquite and wetland habitat along the Colorado River. Use it for a short out-and-back wildlife walk rather than promising a fixed distance. Turn around at the first sign of rising heat, fatigue or low water.

Best behavior: Stay on established routes, give wildlife space and leave plants, nests and river debris where you find them.

2:30–5:00 PM · Cool-down window

Protect the good mood

Return to the hotel, vacation rental or another air-conditioned stop for quiet time. On a cool-weather day, you can instead spend part of this window around Yuma’s historic riverfront, but keep the schedule optional.

5:00–6:30 PM · Dinner

Finish with flexibility

Choose dinner from Yuma’s current restaurant directory based on your family’s appetite and location. A casual Mexican restaurant, burger stop or café can all work; the useful choice is the one that does not require another long wait after an outdoor day.

Summer version: move, shorten or replace

Yuma heat can make a normal-looking itinerary unsafe. In hot weather, move West Wetlands to sunrise or as early as practical, shorten the walk, and skip the East Wetlands afternoon stop. Replace it with an air-conditioned activity or a long indoor rest. Check the National Weather Service forecast and heat alerts for the hours you will be outside.

  • Carry more water than you expect to drink and keep additional water in the vehicle.
  • Use hats, sunscreen, light clothing and frequent cooling breaks.
  • Never wait for dizziness, confusion, vomiting or unusual fatigue before ending the outing.
  • Do not treat shade, river access or a breeze as protection from extreme heat.

What to pack

  • Water for every person plus reserve water
  • Familiar snacks
  • Sun hats and sunscreen
  • Closed-toe walking shoes
  • Cooling towels in warm weather
  • Dry clothes for children
  • Small first-aid kit
  • Binoculars for bird watching
  • Trash bag
  • Downloaded map or screenshots

Bathroom and stroller plan

Make West Wetlands your dependable bathroom checkpoint before lunch or a second outdoor stop. The City’s waterfront map identifies restrooms there, but availability can change. Families using a stroller should begin on developed, paved areas at West Wetlands and avoid assuming that every nature path will be smooth, shaded or step-free.

Frequently asked questions

Is West Wetlands Park good for children?

It can be a strong family stop because it combines walking paths, gardens, picnic areas, a playground, a pond and river access. Adults should closely supervise children around water and adjust the visit for heat.

Are bathrooms available at West Wetlands Park?

The City of Yuma’s waterfront map identifies restrooms at West Wetlands Park. Confirm access when you arrive and make a bathroom stop before moving to the less-developed East Wetlands.

Can we use this itinerary during summer?

Yes, but move outdoor time to the coolest morning hours, shorten or replace it when heat is dangerous, and use an air-conditioned afternoon backup. Carry ample water and do not wait for a child to look ill before ending outdoor activity.

Do we have to complete both parks?

No. West Wetlands alone can fill the useful outdoor part of a family day. East Wetlands is an optional second stop for cool weather and families who still have energy.
