Best Easy Hikes in Arizona for Families With Kids

Start with short, forgiving routes, then choose by season, shade, bathrooms, drive time and the youngest hiker in your group.

By Mark Stephenson, RNArizona father and cardiac catheterization laboratory nurse since 2003Reviewed August 27, 2026

The short version

For a first family hike in Arizona, choose a short trail with simple navigation, an easy turnaround, reliable parking and as much shade or cool-weather margin as you can get. Around Phoenix, Papago Park and White Tank Mountain Regional Park offer several easier options. For warmer months, move uphill toward Payson, the Mogollon Rim, Flagstaff or other high-country destinations rather than trying to make a desert trail behave like air conditioning.

This is not a list of Arizona's most dramatic hikes. It is a list built for families who want a day that ends with kids asking to hike again. The best route is often the one that leaves room for snacks, bathroom breaks, rock collecting, changing weather and an early turnaround.

Easy should describe the trail, not the planning. Arizona heat, elevation and weather still matter.

1. Papago Park, Phoenix

Papago Park is one of the easiest places to introduce kids to Sonoran Desert hiking without committing to a long mountain route. The City of Phoenix rates several Papago trails as easy. Hole-in-the-Rock is listed at 0.2 mile, the Nature Trail at 0.5 mile, and the Crosscut Canal Path at 1.4 miles.

Best for

  • First-time desert hikers
  • Families who want a short outing before another Phoenix activity
  • Kids who need a visible destination rather than an endless trail
  • Parents who want several route lengths in one park

The tradeoff is exposure. A tiny mileage number can become miserable fast in hot weather. For parking, bathrooms, field photos and a realistic family morning, use our Papago Park With Kids guide.

2. Waterfall Trail and Black Rock, White Tank Mountain Regional Park

White Tank Mountain Regional Park is especially useful for West Valley families. Maricopa County lists the Waterfall Trail at 0.9 mile one way, with approximately the first half mile described as barrier-free or accessible. The park also identifies the short Black Rock loop, about 0.5 mile, as an easy walking experience.

The word “waterfall” is not a promise of flowing water. In the desert, water is seasonal and weather-dependent. Go for the canyon, desert scenery, petroglyph-area experience and family walk; treat actual falling water as a bonus rather than the entire sales pitch.

3. Payson and the Mogollon Rim for warmer-weather family walks

When Phoenix heats up, the bigger family decision is often not “which desert trail?” but “should we gain elevation?” Payson and the Mogollon Rim give families pine forest, lakes, picnic stops and short exploratory walks that can be easier to adapt around children than a single exposed desert summit.

Start with our Payson & Mogollon Rim Family Weekend Guide and Woods Canyon Lake With Kids. Those pages use first-party Health & Travels field material and are designed around the whole family day, not just trail mileage.

4. Sedona: choose short segments instead of chasing a famous finish line

Sedona can work beautifully for families when the goal is red-rock scenery rather than checking off a difficult route. Pick a well-used, non-technical trail or pathway, set a turnaround time before you start and be comfortable doing only part of a longer route. Crowding, parking, sun exposure and summer heat can matter more than the published mileage.

Use our Sedona With Kids guide to build the trail around food, rest, transportation and a backup plan instead of forcing the family to fit the trail.

How to choose the right “easy” Arizona hike

Trail ratings are useful, but families need more information than a green circle on a map. Before choosing, compare:

  • Distance: can your youngest hiker comfortably do the return trip too?
  • Elevation gain: short and steep can be harder than longer and flat.
  • Surface: pavement, gravel, packed dirt, loose rock and steps feel very different to children.
  • Exposure: shade can matter more than mileage in Arizona.
  • Bathrooms: know whether they are at the trailhead, elsewhere in the park or nonexistent.
  • Turnaround options: out-and-back routes are easier to shorten than loops.
  • Season and elevation: Phoenix, Sedona, Payson and Flagstaff can have completely different conditions on the same day.

For a deeper decision system, read How to Choose an Arizona Hike for Kids.

Family safety before mileage

Do not use an “easy” rating as a heat-safety rating. Check the exact forecast and official park or forest alerts, carry water for every hiker, set a turnaround time, and stop early when a child is unusually tired, dizzy, nauseated, confused or falling behind.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good easy hike in Phoenix for kids?

Papago Park is a practical first choice. Hole-in-the-Rock is very short, while the Nature Trail and other easy park routes let families adjust the outing without driving somewhere else.

What easy Arizona hike works for families who want a smoother path?

White Tank Mountain Regional Park's Waterfall Trail has a barrier-free section for roughly the first half mile. The park also lists the short Black Rock loop as an easy option.

Are easy Arizona hikes safe in summer?

Not automatically. Easy terrain does not remove heat risk. In hotter months, consider higher-elevation destinations, start with current official conditions and be willing to change plans.
