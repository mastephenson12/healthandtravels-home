Estrella Mountain Regional Park: A Practical West Valley Family Escape

Desert mountains, multi-use trails and family recreation sit close to Goodyear and Avondale. The trick is matching the visit to the season, the group and current park conditions.

By Mark Stephenson, RN · Original on-location photos · Updated August 23, 2026

The short answer

Estrella Mountain Regional Park works well for a cool-season family morning, picnic, short desert outing or longer trail day. The official park site reports approximately 58 miles of multi-use trails ranging from easy to strenuous. In warm weather, visit early and choose a plan that does not depend on hiking far from the vehicle.

Planning a trail day

  1. Download or open the official park map before leaving reliable service.
  2. Choose by the least-experienced person—not the strongest hiker.
  3. Confirm whether the route is shared with bikes and horses.
  4. Set a turnaround time and carry enough water for the return.
  5. Check the park update page for hours, closures, projects and fire restrictions.

Desert safety matters here

  • Avoid exposed hiking during dangerous heat; “just one more mile” is not a cooling strategy.
  • Carry water for every person and do not rely on finding water along the route.
  • Give rattlesnakes, bees and other wildlife space.
  • Watch children closely around cactus, loose slopes and road crossings.
  • Yield appropriately on shared-use trails and announce yourself calmly.

Hours and fees

The park’s official update page currently lists seasonal hours: May 1–October 31 from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m., and November 1–April 30 from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. The photographed board displayed a $10 motorized-vehicle day-use fee and $5 individual walk-in/bicycle/horse fee at the time of this visit. Confirm current hours and fees on the official site because they can change.

Tres Rios Golf Course

Tres Rios Golf Course sits beside the park’s developed area, placing a bright ribbon of green beneath the Estrella Mountains. It is a separate experience from the trail system. Golfers should verify current course operations, tee-time availability and conditions directly before planning around it.

Tres Rios Golf Course beside the desert mountains. Original Health & Travels photo.

A simple half-day family plan

  1. Arrive early. Pay the current entrance fee and confirm park alerts.
  2. Start with one outdoor anchor. Choose a short trail, ranger program or recreation area.
  3. Reset before fatigue. Return for shade, water and food while everyone still feels good.
  4. Keep the second stop optional. Add a scenic drive or nearby activity only if weather and energy cooperate.

Frequently asked questions

Is Estrella Mountain Regional Park good for families?

Yes, when the route and timing match the least-experienced person. The park offers varied recreation, but Sonoran Desert heat and exposure require planning.

Are there easy trails?

The park reports options ranging from easy to strenuous. Use the current official map and ask park staff which signed route best matches your group.

Is Tres Rios Golf Course part of the hiking trails?

No. It is adjacent to the park’s developed area and should be treated as a separate golf experience.

When is the best time to visit?

Cool-season mornings are usually the easiest fit for hiking. In warmer months, begin early and be ready to choose a non-hiking visit.
