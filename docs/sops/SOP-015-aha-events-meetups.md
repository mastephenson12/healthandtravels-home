# SOP 015 — Arizona Hikers Association Events & Meetups

## Purpose
Create safe, useful, repeatable Arizona Hikers Association events that strengthen community, increase retention, and give members a reason to actually get outside together.

## Trigger
Use this SOP whenever AHA plans, announces, hosts, or follows up on a hike, meetup, family outing, workshop, cleanup, ruck, or outdoor event.

## Owner
Arizona Hikers Association Operator.

## Inputs
- Event concept
- Location
- Date and time
- Intended audience
- Difficulty level
- Estimated duration
- Weather/season considerations
- Capacity, if limited
- Host/leader
- Current trail/park access information

## Event Planning Steps
1. Confirm the event serves a clear member purpose: connection, exploration, education, fitness, stewardship, or family activity.
2. Verify the location using current official sources.
3. Confirm access, closures, permits, parking, fees, pets, restrooms, water availability, and applicable rules.
4. Assess family suitability, exposure, shade, elevation, technical difficulty, and bailout/turnaround options.
5. Check forecast and seasonal hazards close to the event date.
6. Assign an event leader and backup contact.
7. Set a clear meeting point and arrival window.
8. Publish realistic expectations rather than promotional bravado.

## Required Event Listing Information
Every event listing should include:
- Event name
- Date and start time
- Meeting location
- Approximate duration
- Distance and difficulty, when applicable
- Elevation gain, when meaningful
- Shade/exposure
- Restroom availability
- Water expectations
- Kid suitability
- Dog rules
- Cost/entry fee
- Required gear
- Weather considerations
- Cancellation policy
- Leader/contact instructions

## Safety Rules
- Never describe a difficult route as beginner-friendly merely to increase attendance.
- Do not rely on members to bring enough water unless the listing explicitly states the requirement.
- Establish a turnaround or cancellation threshold for heat, storms, flash-flood risk, wildfire, road closure, or unsafe trail conditions.
- For monsoon-prone areas, avoid slot canyons, washes, crossings, and flood-prone routes when risk is elevated.
- For high heat, favor early starts, shorter routes, shaded alternatives, or cancellation.
- Make clear that participants remain responsible for their own health, abilities, children, transportation, and personal gear.

## Family Event Standard
When families are invited, include:
- Recommended child age or ability range
- Stroller compatibility, if relevant
- Bathroom information
- Shade and sun exposure
- Expected pace
- Whether early turnaround is easy
- Any steep drop-offs, scrambling, water hazards, or road crossings

## Event Promotion
Promote through appropriate channels:
- AHA community
- Health & Travels newsletter
- Relevant Health & Travels pages
- Sage trip pages
- Social media

Use consistent tracking links where possible.

## Day-Before Check
Within 24 hours of the event:
- Recheck official closures
- Recheck weather
- Recheck fire restrictions or heat alerts
- Confirm leader availability
- Post any last-minute updates
- Cancel or modify if conditions are not appropriate

## Event-Day Procedure
1. Arrive early.
2. Confirm location and conditions.
3. Greet attendees and check that everyone understands the route/plan.
4. Review key safety expectations.
5. Count participants when practical.
6. Keep pace appropriate to the stated audience.
7. Do not pressure anyone to continue.
8. End with a clear return/checkout point.

## After the Event
Within 48 hours:
- Thank participants.
- Invite photos and trip notes.
- Post a short recap.
- Record attendance.
- Note any safety, logistics, or route problems.
- Capture ideas for improving the next event.
- Invite participants to the next relevant AHA activity.

## Quality Check
An event is ready only when:
- Current official access has been checked.
- Difficulty is accurately described.
- Safety and family-fit details are clear.
- Weather/season risks are addressed.
- Meeting instructions are unambiguous.
- A leader is assigned.
- Cancellation/modification rules exist.

## Metrics
Track:
- RSVPs
- Attendance
- Attendance rate
- New members attributed to events
- Repeat attendees
- Community posts/comments after events
- Event-related cancellations/refunds, if any
- Safety or logistics issues

## Success Standard
AHA events should make members feel more confident, connected, and motivated to explore Arizona again. The goal is not to maximize attendance at all costs. A smaller safe event beats a crowded bad idea every time.
