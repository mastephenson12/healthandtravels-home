# SOP 004 — Safety Article

## Purpose
Publish clear, practical Arizona outdoor-safety content that helps families make better decisions before and during an adventure.

## Trigger
A recurring Arizona hazard, seasonal risk, trail-safety topic, incident pattern, or new safety concern warrants a dedicated guide.

## Owner
Health & Travels Content Builder, with elevated fact-checking standards.

## Inputs
- Safety topic
- Intended audience
- Current authoritative sources
- Relevant Arizona locations or conditions
- Related Health & Travels guides
- Relevant Sage planning route

## Priority Topics
Examples include:
- Extreme heat
- Dehydration
- Monsoon storms
- Flash floods
- Lightning
- Wildfire and smoke
- Snow or ice
- Trail closures
- Water crossings
- Wildlife
- Getting lost
- Child pacing and turnaround decisions

## Research Standard
Safety articles require current, authoritative sources whenever conditions, rules, closures, thresholds, or warnings can change.

Prefer sources such as:
- National Weather Service
- National Park Service
- Arizona State Parks
- U.S. Forest Service
- Bureau of Land Management
- County or city parks departments
- Official emergency-management sources

Do not use social posts or secondary summaries as the sole authority for safety-critical claims.

## Article Structure
1. Clear summary of the hazard
2. Who is most at risk
3. What conditions make it worse
4. Warning signs
5. What to do before leaving
6. What to do on the trail or at the destination
7. When to turn around or leave
8. Emergency considerations
9. Official sources
10. Related Health & Travels and Sage links

## Tone
Calm, practical, and specific.

Avoid sensationalism, fear marketing, and fake certainty.

Do not minimize meaningful hazards merely because a location is popular.

## Medical Boundary
Health & Travels may provide general safety and planning information but must not present individualized medical advice.

When health symptoms are discussed:
- Use plain language
- Encourage emergency help when appropriate
- Avoid diagnosis
- Avoid implying certainty from symptoms alone

## Family-Specific Requirements
When relevant, address:
- Children tiring faster than expected
- Limited shade
- Water needs
- Heat exposure
- Child communication and behavior changes
- Turnaround rules
- Backup plans

## Incident References
When discussing deaths, rescues, floods, or other real incidents:
- Verify facts
- Avoid graphic details unless genuinely necessary
- Do not exploit victims for attention
- Use the event only to clarify the safety lesson

## SEO Checklist
- Unique title and meta description
- Canonical URL
- Clear H1
- Descriptive subheadings
- Internal links
- Structured data where appropriate
- Clean slug

## Sage Integration
Use Sage as a planning aid where relevant, especially for:
- Seasonal planning
- Shade
- drive time
- bathrooms
- difficulty
- family fit

## AHA Integration
Use AHA only when community, trail education, or recurring outdoor participation genuinely supports the topic.

## Quality Check
Before publication verify:
- All time-sensitive claims are current
- Authoritative sources are linked
- No unsafe ambiguity
- No unsupported medical claims
- Mobile layout works
- Internal links work
- Sage CTA works if included
- Emergency wording is clear where needed

## Output
A live Health & Travels safety guide.
